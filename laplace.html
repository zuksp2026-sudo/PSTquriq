The Laplace Transform as a Special Case of PST Process–Structure Transition Decomposition

Spartac Cebanu

Abstract. This article interprets the Laplace transform as a concrete engineering realization of the Process–Structure Transition (PST) decomposition. A PST process has the form

$$ Q_1\to U\to R\to I\to Q_2. $$

In the Laplace treatment of a linear time-invariant system, \(Q_1\) is a time-domain input or initial condition, \(U\) is its Laplace transform, \(R\) is the system operator or transfer function, \(I\) is the transformed response, and \(Q_2\) is the recovered time-domain output. The direct and inverse Laplace transforms form the boundary operations of the chain, while \(R\) occupies its structural center.

1. The PST chain

The PST decomposition represents a directed process as

$$ Q_1\to U\to R\to I\to Q_2. $$

In the earlier PST notation, this corresponds to

$$ x\to\nabla F\to F\to F'\to y. $$

2. The Laplace transform

For a suitable function \(q(t)\), the unilateral Laplace transform is

$$ U(s)=\mathcal L\{q(t)\}=\int_{0^-}^{\infty}q(t)e^{-st}\,dt, $$

where \(s=\sigma+j\omega\). The inverse transform recovers the time-domain signal:

$$ q(t)=\mathcal L^{-1}\{U(s)\}. $$

The key property is that differentiation in time becomes multiplication by \(s\):

$$ \mathcal L\{\dot q(t)\}=sU(s)-q(0^-). $$

Thus an initial-value problem involving derivatives becomes an algebraic problem in the complex variable \(s\).

3. PST interpretation

The direct transform is the first PST transition:

$$ Q_1\xrightarrow{\mathcal L}U. $$

It moves from an object to a projection:

$$ \mathcal O\to\mathcal P. $$

The inverse transform is the final transition:

$$ I\xrightarrow{\mathcal L^{-1}}Q_2, $$

returning from a projection to an object:

$$ \mathcal P\to\mathcal O. $$

Between them lies the system structure:

$$ U\to R\to I. $$

Therefore, the Laplace method has the PST form

$$ Q_1\xrightarrow{\mathcal L}U\xrightarrow{R}I\xrightarrow{\mathcal L^{-1}}Q_2. $$

4. The system operator \(R\)

For a linear time-invariant system, \(R\) is represented by a transfer function:

$$ R(s)=G(s)=\frac{Y(s)}{X(s)}. $$

In PST notation,

$$ R(s)=\frac{I(s)}{U(s)}. $$

Hence

$$ I(s)=R(s)U(s), $$

and

$$ Q_2(t)=\mathcal L^{-1}\{I(s)\}. $$

Central PST identity. For a linear time-invariant system,

$$ Q_2(t)=\mathcal L^{-1}\left[R(s)\,\mathcal L\{Q_1(t)\}\right]. $$

In PST notation,

$$ Q_1\to U\to R\to I\to Q_2. $$

5. Example: an RC circuit

For a first-order RC low-pass filter,

$$ RC\frac{dy}{dt}+y=x. $$

Taking the Laplace transform with zero initial conditions gives

$$ (RCs+1)Y(s)=X(s). $$

Therefore,

$$ R(s)=\frac{1}{RCs+1}, $$

and

$$ I(s)=\frac{U(s)}{RCs+1}. $$

Finally,

$$ Q_2(t)=\mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\frac{U(s)}{RCs+1}\right\}. $$

This is exactly the PST sequence:

$$ Q_1\to U\to R\to I\to Q_2. $$

7. The palindrome

The Laplace cycle has the structure

$$ \text{time}\to\text{frequency}\to\text{frequency}\to\text{time}. $$

More precisely,

$$ Q_1\to U\to I\to Q_2, $$

with the operator \(R\) at the center:

$$ Q_1\to U\to R\to I\to Q_2. $$

The outer transitions leave and return to the time domain. The inner transitions remain in the frequency domain but change the role of the spectral object: first, the input spectrum is connected to the operator; then, the operator produces the output spectrum.

Interpretation. The palindrome expresses a controlled detour: the process leaves the time domain, passes through a structural operator, and returns with a new computed outcome.

8. Why it works

The power of the Laplace transform comes from

$$ \text{differentiation in time}\longleftrightarrow\text{multiplication by }s. $$

Therefore,

$$ \text{differential equation}\longrightarrow\text{algebraic equation}. $$

The dynamic problem is temporarily projected into a domain where it can be manipulated algebraically, then projected back.

This is the PST principle:

$$ \text{object}\to\text{projection}\to\text{structure}\to\text{projection}\to\text{object}. $$

9. Boundary of the analogy

The identification is strongest for linear, time-invariant, single-input single-output systems, where \(R\) is a genuine transfer function. For nonlinear, time-varying, distributed-parameter, or stochastic systems, \(R\) may depend on state, time, amplitude, frequency, or boundary conditions.

Thus,

$$ R=\text{transfer function} $$

is a special case of

$$ R=\text{process-structure operator}. $$

10. Conclusion

The Laplace transform is a concrete realization of the PST structure:

$$ Q_1\to U\to R\to I\to Q_2. $$

The direct transform opens the boundary between time and frequency; the system operator transforms the spectral representation; the inverse transform closes the boundary and returns a new time-domain object.

$$ \boxed{Q_2=\mathcal L^{-1}\left[R(s)\,\mathcal L\{Q_1(t)\}\right].} $$
