Abstract

This document states the strongest defensible form of the Process–Structure Transition (PST) decomposition. It does not claim that every conceivable process has exactly five nodes and four transitions. Instead, it proves that every process in a precisely defined class of deterministic scalar dynamical systems admits a canonical PST realization

\[x\longrightarrow\nabla F\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow V_F\longrightarrow y.\]

The chain has five nodes and four transitions. Its existence follows from the potential representation of a first-order ordinary differential equation and the Picard–Lindelöf theorem. Its minimality is proved relative to explicit PST axioms, not as an unrestricted law of nature.

1. Scope and main claim

Let a process be a deterministic evolution from an initial state \(x\) to a realized trajectory \(y\). The unrestricted statement

\[\text{every process }x\to y\text{ decomposes into five nodes and four transitions}\]

is not a mathematical theorem. It is an empirical or metaphysical claim. The provable PST statement is conditional:

\[\text{every PST-representable deterministic process admits the canonical five-node decomposition.}\]

The central PST chain is

\[x\longrightarrow\nabla F\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow V_F\longrightarrow y.\]

Here \(V_F\) is an independent field of potential change. It must not be defined as \(dF(y(t),t)/dt\) if it is then used to reconstruct \(y(t)\); that would create a circular dependency.

2. PST categories and operators

Let \(\mathcal O\) be the category of objects: initial states, structures, and realized trajectories. Let \(\mathcal P\) be the category of projections: gradients, vector fields, functional descriptions, and dynamical representations.

Let

\[\Phi_z:\mathcal O\longrightarrow\mathcal P,\qquad\Phi_o:\mathcal P\longrightarrow\mathcal O\]

be the forgetting and freeing functors. A PST functorial trajectory is defined by

\[U=\Phi_z(Q_1),\qquad R=\Phi_o(U),\qquad I=\Phi_z(R),\qquad Q_2=\Phi_o(I).\]

Therefore,

\[Q_2=(\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z)(Q_1).\]

The node-type pattern is

\[\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O.\]

The adjunction \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\), if assumed, provides

\[\eta:\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal P}\Longrightarrow\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o,\qquad\varepsilon:\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal O},\]

and the natural bijection

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Phi_oP,O)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(P,\Phi_zO).\]

However, the adjunction does not by itself prove that every process has exactly five nodes. That statement requires the PST axioms below.

3. Scalar dynamical arena

Let \(\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R\) be open and let \(I=[t_0,t_f]\). Consider the scalar Cauchy problem

\[\dot y(t)=G(y(t),t),\qquad y(t_0)=x\in\Omega.\]

Assume that there exist functions \(F\in C^2(\Omega\times I)\) and \(V_F\in C^1(\Omega\times I)\) such that

\[G(y,t)=\frac{V_F(y,t)-\partial_tF(y,t)}{\nabla F(y,t)},\qquad\nabla F(y,t)\neq0.\]

Then the process is called PST-representable.

4. PST realization theorem

Theorem 4.1 — PST realization. Every PST-representable scalar deterministic process admits a canonical PST decomposition

\[x\longrightarrow\nabla F\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow V_F\longrightarrow y.\]

The decomposition has exactly five nodes and four transitions.

Proof. By the PST-representability condition,

\[\dot y(t)=\frac{V_F(y(t),t)-\partial_tF(y(t),t)}{\nabla F(y(t),t)}.\]

Define

\[Q_1=x,\qquad U=\nabla F,\qquad R=F,\qquad I=V_F,\qquad Q_2=y.\]

Then the chain

\[x\longrightarrow\nabla F\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow V_F\longrightarrow y\]

has five nodes and four transitions. Since the right-hand side is locally Lipschitz in \(y\), the Picard–Lindelöf theorem gives a unique local solution satisfying \(y(t_0)=x\). Thus the final node \(y\) is not an additional assumption: it is the integral curve generated by the preceding PST data. \(\square\)

5. Minimality theorem

Theorem 5.1 — PST grammatical minimality. Assume that a PST-compatible connected process satisfies the following axioms:

  1. It begins and ends in \(\mathsf O\).
  2. Adjacent nodes have different types.
  3. It contains a structure \(R\) distinct from the initial and terminal states.
  4. It contains a realization \(I\) distinct from \(R\).
  5. Direct transitions \(\mathsf O\to\mathsf O\) and \(\mathsf P\to\mathsf P\) are forbidden.
  6. Removal of any node destroys deterministic reconstructibility of the terminal trajectory.

Then every such process has at least five nodes and four transitions. If it is defined as a PST functorial trajectory, it has exactly five nodes and four transitions.

Proof. Because the process begins and ends in \(\mathsf O\), and adjacent nodes must alternate types, its type sequence must have the form

\[\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O.\]

A shorter alternating sequence,

\[\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O,\]

contains only one intermediate object. It cannot simultaneously contain a structure \(R\) distinct from the endpoints and a realization \(I\) distinct from \(R\). Hence at least four transitions are required. Four transitions generate exactly five nodes. \(\square\)

6. Gauge equivalence

Theorem 6.1 — PST gauge preservation. Let

\[G(y,t)=\frac{\varphi(y,t)}{M(y,t)},\qquad M(y,t)>0.\]

Assume locally that \(\nabla F(y,t)>0\), and define

\[M(y,t)=\frac{1}{\nabla F(y,t)},\qquad\varphi(y,t)=\frac{G(y,t)}{\nabla F(y,t)}.\]

Then

\[\frac{\varphi(y,t)}{M(y,t)}=G(y,t).\]

Moreover, the rescaling

\[(M,\varphi)\mapsto(\lambda M,\lambda\varphi),\qquad\lambda>0,\]

corresponds to the potential rescaling

\[F\mapsto\frac{F}{\lambda},\]

and leaves the trajectory \(y(t)\) invariant.

Proof. We compute

\[\frac{\varphi}{M}=\frac{G/\nabla F}{1/\nabla F}=G.\]

If \(F\mapsto F/\lambda\), then \(\nabla F\mapsto(1/\lambda)\nabla F\). Hence

\[M\mapsto\lambda M,\qquad\varphi\mapsto\lambda\varphi.\]

Therefore \(\varphi/M=G\) is unchanged, and the solution \(y(t)\) is unchanged. \(\square\)

8. What is not claimed

The theorem does not assert that every conceivable process is PST-representable. It does not assert that every process must have exactly five nodes. It does not derive the five-node structure from the adjunction alone.

The exact result is:

\[\text{PST-representable process}\Longrightarrow\text{canonical five-node PST decomposition}.\]

The converse is false without additional axioms: a five-node diagram alone does not guarantee that an arbitrary empirical process is PST-representable.

9. Final ALVK statement

Final statement. For every PST-representable deterministic process, the evolution

\[x\longrightarrow y\]

admits the canonical decomposition

\[x\longrightarrow\nabla F\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow V_F\longrightarrow y.\]

The decomposition has five nodes and four transitions. Its existence follows from the potential representation of the governing ODE and Picard–Lindelöf local existence and uniqueness. Its minimality follows from the PST axioms of type alternation, distinct structure and realization, and deterministic reconstructibility.
