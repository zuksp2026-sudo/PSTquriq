PST as a Categorical Operator Apparatus

Final ALVK Version

Spartac Cebanu

November 17, 2026

Abstract

This document gives a final categorical formulation of the Process–Structure Transition (PST) as an operator apparatus. PST is represented by the five-node functorial trajectory

\[Q_1\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow Q_2,\]

with node types

\[\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O.\]

The categorical core proves the functor-composition identity

\[Q_2=(\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z)(Q_1).\]

The adjunction \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\) provides the unit \(\eta\), the counit \(\varepsilon\), triangle identities, and a Hom-set bijection. The document then adds the operator-level PST axioms needed for practical use: the comma-category of clothings, the minimal structure \(R_{\min}\), the conductivity condition \(R\to I\), an engineering minimality functional, and a scalar dynamical realization. The meso-pattern \(\varepsilon\to\eta\to\eta\to\varepsilon\) is treated as a separate PST hypothesis, not as a consequence of the adjunction.

Purpose

PST is not introduced here as a claim that every process must have exactly five nodes. Rather, PST is introduced as a categorical operator apparatus: a fixed grammar for representing a process as a structured trajectory.

The basic PST chain is

\[Q_1\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow Q_2.\]

In the functorial formulation, the chain is generated by alternating applications of two functors:

\[\Phi_z;\Phi_o;\Phi_z;\Phi_o.\]

The semicolon notation denotes left-to-right application:

\[(\Phi_z;\Phi_o;\Phi_z;\Phi_o)(X)=\Phi_o(\Phi_z(\Phi_o(\Phi_z(X)))).\]

Equivalently,

\[(\Phi_z;\Phi_o;\Phi_z;\Phi_o)(X)=(\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z)(X).\]

PST categories

Definition — PST categories

Let \(\mathcal O\) be the category of objects: material configurations, structures, accumulated states, or realized products.

Let \(\mathcal P\) be the category of projections: gradients, vectors, functional descriptions, constraints, or dynamical representations.

Definition — PST functors

Let

\[\Phi_z:\mathcal O\longrightarrow\mathcal P\]

be the forgetting functor, which extracts a projection from an object.

Let

\[\Phi_o:\mathcal P\longrightarrow\mathcal O\]

be the freeing functor, which constructs an object from a projection.

Remark

The notation is fixed as follows:

\[\Phi_z=\text{forgetting functor},\qquad\Phi_o=\text{freeing functor}.\]

Thus,

\[\Phi_z:\mathcal O\to\mathcal P,\qquad\Phi_o:\mathcal P\to\mathcal O.\]

PST functorial trajectory

Axiom — PST functorial trajectory

A PST process is a directed chain

\[Q_1\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow Q_2\]

defined by the functorial equations

\[U=\Phi_z(Q_1),\]
\[R=\Phi_o(U),\]
\[I=\Phi_z(R),\]
\[Q_2=\Phi_o(I).\]

Remark

This axiom is stronger than merely saying that

\[Q_1,R,Q_2\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal O),\qquad U,I\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal P).\]

It says that every node is obtained from the previous one by a fixed functor. Therefore, PST is not merely a typed graph; it is a functorial trajectory.

Lemma — Node typing

In a PST functorial trajectory,

\[Q_1,R,Q_2\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal O),\qquad U,I\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal P).\]

Proof

Since \(\Phi_z:\mathcal O\to\mathcal P\) and \(Q_1\in\mathcal O\), we have

\[U=\Phi_z(Q_1)\in\mathcal P.\]

Since \(\Phi_o:\mathcal P\to\mathcal O\) and \(U\in\mathcal P\), we have

\[R=\Phi_o(U)\in\mathcal O.\]

Since \(\Phi_z:\mathcal O\to\mathcal P\) and \(R\in\mathcal O\), we have

\[I=\Phi_z(R)\in\mathcal P.\]

Since \(\Phi_o:\mathcal P\to\mathcal O\) and \(I\in\mathcal P\), we have

\[Q_2=\Phi_o(I)\in\mathcal O.\]

Therefore,

\[Q_1,R,Q_2\in\mathcal O,\qquad U,I\in\mathcal P.\]

Corollary — PST node-type alternation

Every PST functorial trajectory has node types

\[\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O.\]

Proof

By the node-typing lemma,

\[Q_1,R,Q_2\in\mathcal O,\qquad U,I\in\mathcal P.\]

Therefore,

\[\operatorname{type}(Q_1)=\mathsf O,\qquad\operatorname{type}(U)=\mathsf P,\qquad\operatorname{type}(R)=\mathsf O,\qquad\operatorname{type}(I)=\mathsf P,\qquad\operatorname{type}(Q_2)=\mathsf O.\]

Hence,

\[Q_1-U-R-I-Q_2=\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O.\]

PST composition

Theorem — PST functor-composition theorem

Assume that PST is a functorial trajectory:

\[U=\Phi_z(Q_1),\qquad R=\Phi_o(U),\qquad I=\Phi_z(R),\qquad Q_2=\Phi_o(I).\]

Then

\[Q_2=(\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z)(Q_1).\]

Proof

By the PST functorial-trajectory axiom,

\[U=\Phi_z(Q_1).\]

Therefore,

\[R=\Phi_o(U)=\Phi_o(\Phi_z(Q_1)).\]

Therefore,

\[I=\Phi_z(R)=\Phi_z(\Phi_o(\Phi_z(Q_1))).\]

Therefore,

\[Q_2=\Phi_o(I)=\Phi_o(\Phi_z(\Phi_o(\Phi_z(Q_1)))).\]

Using functor composition, this is

\[Q_2=(\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z)(Q_1).\]

Theorem — PST operator-pattern theorem

Assume that PST is a functorial trajectory. Then the operator pattern of PST is the sequence of functor applications

\[\Phi_z;\Phi_o;\Phi_z;\Phi_o.\]

Equivalently, the PST chain is generated by the alternating application

\[\Phi_z,\qquad\Phi_o,\qquad\Phi_z,\qquad\Phi_o.\]

Proof

By the PST functorial-trajectory axiom,

\[U=\Phi_z(Q_1),\]
\[R=\Phi_o(U),\]
\[I=\Phi_z(R),\]
\[Q_2=\Phi_o(I).\]

Therefore, the first transition is the application of \(\Phi_z\) to \(Q_1\):

\[Q_1\mapsto U=\Phi_z(Q_1).\]

The second transition is the application of \(\Phi_o\) to \(U\):

\[U\mapsto R=\Phi_o(U).\]

The third transition is the application of \(\Phi_z\) to \(R\):

\[R\mapsto I=\Phi_z(R).\]

The fourth transition is the application of \(\Phi_o\) to \(I\):

\[I\mapsto Q_2=\Phi_o(I).\]

Hence, the PST process has operator pattern

\[\Phi_z;\Phi_o;\Phi_z;\Phi_o.\]

Remark — Correct reading of the PST chain

The expression

\[Q_1\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow Q_2\]

is a chain of object-to-object transitions.

The expression

\[\Phi_z;\Phi_o;\Phi_z;\Phi_o\]

is the sequence of functors generating those transitions.

Therefore, the following expression is not a categorical identity between objects and functors:

\[Q_1\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow Q_2=\Phi_z\longrightarrow\Phi_o\longrightarrow\Phi_z\longrightarrow\Phi_o.\]

The correct statement is:

\[Q_2=(\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z)(Q_1).\]

Alternatively, one may say that the PST chain has operator pattern

\[\Phi_z;\Phi_o;\Phi_z;\Phi_o.\]

Adjunction

Definition — Adjunction

Let

\[\Phi_o:\mathcal P\to\mathcal O,\qquad\Phi_z:\mathcal O\to\mathcal P.\]

An adjunction

\[\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\]

consists of natural transformations

\[\eta:\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal P}\Longrightarrow\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o,\]
\[\varepsilon:\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal O},\]

such that the triangle identities hold.

Proposition — Unit and counit

If \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\), then:

\[\eta:\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal P}\Longrightarrow\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o,\]
\[\varepsilon:\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal O}.\]

Proof

This is part of the definition of an adjunction. For an adjunction \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\), the unit is

\[\eta:\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal P}\Longrightarrow\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o,\]

and the counit is

\[\varepsilon:\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal O}.\]

Thus \(\eta\) and \(\varepsilon\) are canonical natural transformations associated with the adjunction.

Proposition — Triangle identities

If \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\), then for every \(P\in\mathcal P\) and \(O\in\mathcal O\),

\[\varepsilon_{\Phi_o(P)}\circ\Phi_o(\eta_P)=\operatorname{id}_{\Phi_o(P)},\]
\[\Phi_z(\varepsilon_O)\circ\eta_{\Phi_z(O)}=\operatorname{id}_{\Phi_z(O)}.\]

Proof

These are the standard triangle identities of an adjunction. For an adjunction \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\), the unit and counit satisfy:

\[(\varepsilon\Phi_o)\circ(\Phi_o\eta)=\operatorname{id}_{\Phi_o},\]
\[(\Phi_z\varepsilon)\circ(\eta\Phi_z)=\operatorname{id}_{\Phi_z}.\]

Equivalently, for every \(P\in\mathcal P\) and \(O\in\mathcal O\):

\[\varepsilon_{\Phi_o(P)}\circ\Phi_o(\eta_P)=\operatorname{id}_{\Phi_o(P)},\]
\[\Phi_z(\varepsilon_O)\circ\eta_{\Phi_z(O)}=\operatorname{id}_{\Phi_z(O)}.\]

Proposition — Hom-set bijection

If \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\), then for every \(P\in\mathcal P\) and \(O\in\mathcal O\) there is a natural bijection

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Phi_o(P),O)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(P,\Phi_z(O)).\]

Proof

By the hom-set definition of adjunction, \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\) means that for every \(P\in\mathcal P\) and \(O\in\mathcal O\) there is a natural bijection

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Phi_o(P),O)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(P,\Phi_z(O)).\]

This is the standard form:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D}(F(X),Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(X,G(Y)),\]

where

\[F=\Phi_o:\mathcal P\to\mathcal O,\qquad G=\Phi_z:\mathcal O\to\mathcal P.\]

Therefore:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Phi_o(P),O)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(P,\Phi_z(O)).\]

Remark — Correct PST substitution

Taking \(P=U\) and \(O=Q_1\), the Hom-set bijection gives:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Phi_o(U),Q_1)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(U,\Phi_z(Q_1)).\]

Since \(R=\Phi_o(U)\), this becomes:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(R,Q_1)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(U,\Phi_z(Q_1)).\]

Since \(\Phi_z(Q_1)=U\), this may also be written as:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(R,Q_1)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(U,U).\]

This is a formal Hom-set correspondence; it does not by itself assert a physical current or a causal relation from \(R\) to \(Q_1\).

Taking \(P=I\) and \(O=R\), the Hom-set bijection gives:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Phi_o(I),R)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(I,\Phi_z(R)).\]

Since \(Q_2=\Phi_o(I)\), this becomes:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(Q_2,R)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(I,\Phi_z(R)).\]

Since \(I=\Phi_z(R)\), this may also be written as:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(Q_2,R)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(I,I).\]

Thus the correct PST Hom-correspondences are:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(R,Q_1)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(U,U),\]
\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(Q_2,R)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(I,I).\]

Epsilon, eta, and PST edges

Remark — Level distinction

The operators \(\eta\) and \(\varepsilon\) are natural transformations:

\[\eta:\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal P}\Longrightarrow\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o,\]
\[\varepsilon:\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal O}.\]

Therefore, they are not morphisms of the PST chain and cannot be identified with its edges.

The PST chain consists of applications of functors:

\[\Phi_z;\Phi_o;\Phi_z;\Phi_o.\]

The operators \(\eta\) and \(\varepsilon\) live between composites of these functors.

Remark — PST operator assignment

In PST, the following assignment is used as a labeling convention:

\[\varepsilon\leftrightarrow\Phi_z,\qquad\eta\leftrightarrow\Phi_o.\]
  • \(\varepsilon\) is associated with the forgetting functor \(\Phi_z\).
  • \(\eta\) is associated with the freeing functor \(\Phi_o\).

However, categorically, both \(\eta\) and \(\varepsilon\) depend on both functors:

\[\eta:\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal P}\Longrightarrow\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o,\]
\[\varepsilon:\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal O}.\]

Therefore, the PST assignment is a mnemonic or labeling convention, not a categorical identity.

Comma-category and minimal structure

Definition — Category of clothings

Let \(u\in\mathcal P\). The comma-category

\[(u\downarrow\Phi_z)\]

has as objects pairs

\[(X,f),\]

where

\[X\in\mathcal O,\qquad f:u\to\Phi_zX.\]

A morphism

\[h:(X,f)\longrightarrow(Y,g)\]

is a morphism

\[h:X\to Y\]

in \(\mathcal O\) such that the diagram

\[\begin{array}{ccc}u&\xrightarrow{f}&\Phi_zX\\&{}_{g}\searrow&\downarrow{\Phi_zh}\\&&\Phi_zY\end{array}\]

commutes.

Theorem — Initial clothing

Assume that \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\). Then for every \(u\in\mathcal P\), the object

\[(\Phi_ou,\eta_u)\]

is an initial object in the comma-category

\[(u\downarrow\Phi_z).\]

Proof

By the Hom-set bijection of the adjunction,

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Phi_ou,X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(u,\Phi_zX).\]

Therefore, for every object \((X,f)\in(u\downarrow\Phi_z)\), there is a unique morphism

\[\Phi_ou\to X\]

corresponding to \(f:u\to\Phi_zX\).

Hence, \((\Phi_ou,\eta_u)\) is initial in \((u\downarrow\Phi_z)\).

Definition — Minimal PST structure

Assume that \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\). For a projection \(u\in\mathcal P\), the minimal PST structure is

\[R_{\min}=\Phi_ou.\]

Remark

Categorically, \(R_{\min}\) is the initial clothing of the projection \(u\). It is universal in the comma-category \((u\downarrow\Phi_z)\).

The statement that \(R_{\min}\) is also minimal in an engineering sense requires an additional PST minimality axiom or a specified cost functional.

Conductivity

Axiom — PST conductivity

A PST structure \(R\) is conductive to a projection \(I\) if there exists a nonzero admissible transition

\[R\longrightarrow I.\]

The conductivity is denoted

\[\operatorname{Conduct}(R,I)>0.\]

Remark

The conductivity condition is a PST axiom, not a consequence of the adjunction.

A Hom-morphism may exist formally without representing a physical current, a causal relation, or an engineering flow. Therefore, PST distinguishes:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(R,I)\neq\operatorname{Conduct}(R,I).\]

Definition — Conductive PST trajectory

A PST functorial trajectory is conductive if

\[\operatorname{Conduct}(R,I)>0.\]

Remark

The \(R\to I\) transition is the operational center of PST. It tests whether the assembled structure can sustain the required flow.

The adjunction provides the formal Hom-correspondence:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Phi_o(P),O)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(P,\Phi_z(O)).\]

The conductivity axiom adds the physical or engineering condition that the corresponding transition is realizable.

Engineering minimality

Definition — PST cost functional

Let \(R\in\mathcal O\). A PST cost functional is a map

\[J:\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal O)\longrightarrow\mathbb R\]

of the form

\[J(R)=\alpha C(R)+\beta D(R)+\gamma L(R)-\delta P(R),\]
  • \(C(R)\) is the resource cost;
  • \(D(R)\) is the dissipation or friction;
  • \(L(R)\) is the structural complexity;
  • \(P(R)\) is the useful conductivity or productive capacity;
  • \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\ge0\).

Axiom — PST engineering minimality

A PST structure \(R\) is engineering-minimal for a projection \(u\) if

\[R=R_{\min}\qquad\text{and}\qquad R_{\min}=\arg\min_{R\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal O)}J(R).\]

Remark

The categorical initial-object property and the engineering-minimality property are distinct:

\[R_{\min}=\Phi_ou\]

is categorical minimality, whereas

\[R_{\min}=\arg\min_RJ(R)\]

is engineering minimality.

PST becomes a strong engineering theory when these two notions are shown to coincide for a specified class of cost functionals.

Hypothesis — PST minimality compatibility

For a specified class of PST cost functionals \(J\), the categorical minimal clothing \(R_{\min}=\Phi_ou\) satisfies

\[R_{\min}=\arg\min_RJ(R).\]

Scalar dynamical realization

Definition — Scalar PST data

Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R\) be an open domain and let \(I=[t_0,t_f]\). Consider a scalar Cauchy problem

\[\dot y(t)=G(y(t),t),\qquad y(t_0)=x\in\Omega.\]

The scalar PST data are

\[\mathcal S_F=(x,\nabla F,F,F',y),\]
  • \(x\in\Omega\) is the initial state;
  • \(F\in C^2(\Omega\times I)\) is a scalar potential;
  • \(\nabla F(y,t)=\partial_yF(y,t)\) is the spatial gradient;
  • \(F'(y,t)\) is an independent scalar field of potential change;
  • \(y\in C^1(I,\Omega)\) is the realized trajectory.

Axiom — Nondegeneracy

The scalar PST data satisfy

\[\nabla F(y,t)\neq0\qquad\text{on }\Omega\times I.\]

Theorem — PST dynamics theorem

Assume that \(F\in C^2(\Omega\times I)\), that \(\nabla F(y,t)\neq0\), and that \(F'(y,t)\) is an independent scalar field. Define

\[\dot y(t)=\frac{F'(y(t),t)-\partial_tF(y(t),t)}{\nabla F(y(t),t)}.\]

If the right-hand side is locally Lipschitz in \(y\), then the Cauchy problem has a unique local solution with initial condition \(y(t_0)=x\).

Proof

The equation is an ordinary differential equation of the form

\[\dot y(t)=H(y(t),t),\]

where

\[H(y,t)=\frac{F'(y,t)-\partial_tF(y,t)}{\nabla F(y,t)}.\]

Since \(\nabla F(y,t)\neq0\), the denominator is nonzero. Since the right-hand side is locally Lipschitz in \(y\), the Picard–Lindelöf theorem gives local existence and uniqueness of the solution satisfying

\[y(t_0)=x.\]

Remark

The field \(F'(y,t)\) must be specified independently of the trajectory \(y(t)\). It cannot be defined as

\[F'(t)=\frac{d}{dt}F(y(t),t)\]

if it is then used to reconstruct \(y(t)\). That would create a circular dependency.

The correct interpretation is:

\[F'(y,t)=\text{given rate of potential change at state }y\text{ and time }t.\]

Gauge equivalence

Definition — PST gauge pair

Assume that the governing vector field admits a decomposition

\[G(y,t)=\frac{\varphi(y,t)}{M(y,t)},\qquad M(y,t)>0.\]

Assume locally that

\[\nabla F(y,t)>0.\]

Define

\[M(y,t)=\frac{1}{\nabla F(y,t)},\qquad\varphi(y,t)=\frac{G(y,t)}{\nabla F(y,t)}.\]

Proposition — Gauge preservation

With the definitions above,

\[\frac{\varphi(y,t)}{M(y,t)}=G(y,t).\]

Proof

We compute:

\[\frac{\varphi(y,t)}{M(y,t)}=\frac{G(y,t)/\nabla F(y,t)}{1/\nabla F(y,t)}=G(y,t).\]

Proposition — Scale correspondence

Let

\[(M,\varphi)\mapsto(\lambda M,\lambda\varphi),\qquad\lambda>0.\]

Then this transformation corresponds to the potential rescaling

\[F\mapsto F/\lambda.\]

Proof

If

\[F\mapsto F/\lambda,\]

then

\[\nabla F\mapsto\frac{1}{\lambda}\nabla F.\]

Therefore,

\[M=\frac{1}{\nabla F}\mapsto\lambda M,\]

and

\[\varphi=\frac{G}{\nabla F}\mapsto\lambda\varphi.\]

Hence,

\[(M,\varphi)\mapsto(\lambda M,\lambda\varphi).\]

Remark

The trajectory \(y(t)\) is invariant under the gauge transformation

\[(M,\varphi)\mapsto(\lambda M,\lambda\varphi),\]

because

\[\frac{\varphi}{M}=G\]

is unchanged.

A full proof of gauge invariance as a natural isomorphism requires an explicit action of \(\lambda\) on all intermediate PST objects and a verification of naturality for all morphisms.

Meso-level hypothesis

Hypothesis — Meso-transition pattern

Each PST edge may admit an internal meso-level unfolding of the form

\[\varepsilon\to\eta\to\eta\to\varepsilon.\]

Remark

This pattern is not a consequence of the adjunction \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\).

In particular, \(\eta\) and \(\varepsilon\) are natural transformations, not ordinary morphisms of the PST chain. Therefore, the expression

\[\varepsilon\to\eta\to\eta\to\varepsilon\]

must be interpreted at a separate meso-level, with its own objects, morphisms, composition rule, and boundary geometry.

The meso-pattern is a PST hypothesis or axiom, not a categorical identity.

What is not proved

Remark — What is not proved

The preceding theorems do not prove the following without additional PST axioms or empirical hypotheses:

  • That every process must have exactly five nodes and four edges.
  • That \(Q_1\) must be raw material and \(Q_2\) must be an autonomous product.
  • That \(R\) must be minimal in an engineering sense.
  • That polarity must be \(\mathrm{off}-\mathrm{on}-\mathrm{on}-\mathrm{off}\).
  • That every edge must internally unfold as \(\varepsilon\to\eta\to\eta\to\varepsilon\).
  • That the existence of a Hom-morphism necessarily implies a physical current.
  • That the categorical initial object \(R_{\min}\) necessarily minimizes every engineering cost functional.

These require separate PST axioms, domain-specific models, or empirical verification.

Operator protocol

Protocol — PST operator protocol

To apply PST to a concrete process, proceed as follows:

  1. Define the category of objects \(\mathcal O\).
  2. Define the category of projections \(\mathcal P\).
  3. Define the forgetting functor \(\Phi_z:\mathcal O\to\mathcal P\).
  4. Define the freeing functor \(\Phi_o:\mathcal P\to\mathcal O\).
  5. Fix the initial object \(Q_1\in\mathcal O\).
  6. Compute the projection
\[U=\Phi_z(Q_1).\]
  1. Construct the minimal structure
\[R_{\min}=\Phi_ou,\qquad u=U.\]
  1. Test conductivity:
\[\operatorname{Conduct}(R_{\min},I)>0.\]
  1. Compute the flux
\[I=\Phi_z(R_{\min}).\]
  1. Compute the final object
\[Q_2=\Phi_o(I).\]
  1. Verify that \(Q_2\) satisfies the required observable outcome.

Main result

Theorem — Final PST operator theorem

Assume:

  1. \(\mathcal O\) and \(\mathcal P\) are categories.
  2. \(\Phi_z:\mathcal O\to\mathcal P\) and \(\Phi_o:\mathcal P\to\mathcal O\) are functors.
  3. PST is a functorial trajectory:
\[U=\Phi_z(Q_1),\qquad R=\Phi_o(U),\qquad I=\Phi_z(R),\qquad Q_2=\Phi_o(I).\]
  1. \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\) is an adjunction.
  2. The \(R\to I\) transition is conductive:
\[\operatorname{Conduct}(R,I)>0.\]

Then:

\[Q_2=(\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z)(Q_1).\]

Furthermore, the PST process has operator pattern

\[\Phi_z;\Phi_o;\Phi_z;\Phi_o.\]

Moreover, the adjunction provides:

\[\eta:\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal P}\Longrightarrow\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o,\]
\[\varepsilon:\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal O},\]

and the Hom-set bijection:

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Phi_o(P),O)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(P,\Phi_z(O)).\]

Finally, the minimal PST structure is

\[R_{\min}=\Phi_ou,\qquad u=U,\]

and it is initial in the comma-category

\[(u\downarrow\Phi_z).\]

Proof

By the PST functorial-trajectory axiom,

\[U=\Phi_z(Q_1),\]
\[R=\Phi_o(U),\]
\[I=\Phi_z(R),\]
\[Q_2=\Phi_o(I).\]

Substituting successively,

\[R=\Phi_o(\Phi_z(Q_1)),\]
\[I=\Phi_z(\Phi_o(\Phi_z(Q_1))),\]
\[Q_2=\Phi_o(\Phi_z(\Phi_o(\Phi_z(Q_1)))).\]

Therefore,

\[Q_2=(\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z)(Q_1).\]

Thus, the PST functor-composition theorem is proved.

By the adjunction \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\), there exist:

\[\eta:\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal P}\Longrightarrow\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o,\]
\[\varepsilon:\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal O}.\]

Moreover, by the Hom-set characterization of the adjunction,

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Phi_o(P),O)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(P,\Phi_z(O))\]

for every \(P\in\mathcal P\) and \(O\in\mathcal O\).

Since \(u=U=\Phi_z(Q_1)\), the minimal PST structure is

\[R_{\min}=\Phi_ou.\]

By the initial-object theorem for the comma-category \((u\downarrow\Phi_z)\), the pair

\[(\Phi_ou,\eta_u)\]

is initial in \((u\downarrow\Phi_z)\).

Therefore, the final PST operator theorem is proved.
