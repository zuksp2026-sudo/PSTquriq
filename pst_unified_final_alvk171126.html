Abstract

This document establishes the strongest defensible form of the Process–Structure Transition (PST) decomposition. It does not claim that every conceivable process has exactly five nodes and four transitions. Instead, it proves that every process in a precisely defined class of deterministic dynamical systems admits a canonical PST realization:

\[x\longrightarrow(dE,dS)\longrightarrow(L,M)\longrightarrow v\longrightarrow y.\]

The chain has five nodes and four transitions. Its existence follows from the potential representation of a first-order ordinary differential equation, the GENERIC structure of nonequilibrium dynamics, and the Picard–Lindelöf theorem. Its minimality is proved relative to explicit PST axioms, not as an unrestricted law of nature. Its uniqueness is relative to a fixed PST representation.

1. Scope and main claim

Let a process be a deterministic evolution from an initial state \(x\) to a realized trajectory \(y\). The unrestricted statement

\[\text{every process }x\to y\text{ decomposes into five nodes and four transitions}\]

is not a mathematical theorem. It is an empirical or metaphysical claim. The provable PST statement is conditional:

\[\text{every PST-representable deterministic process admits the canonical five-node decomposition.}\]

The central PST chain is

\[x\longrightarrow(dE,dS)\longrightarrow(L,M)\longrightarrow v\longrightarrow y.\]

Here \(dE\) is the differential of an energy-like potential, \(dS\) is the differential of an entropy-like potential, \((L,M)\) is the pair of reversible and irreversible structure operators, \(v\) is the induced flow, and \(y\) is the realized trajectory.

2. PST categories and operators

Let \(\mathcal O\) be the category of objects: initial states, structures, and realized trajectories. Let \(\mathcal P\) be the category of phase carriers: covectors, vector fields, functional descriptions, and dynamical representations.

Let

\[\Phi_z:\mathcal O\longrightarrow\mathcal P,\qquad\Phi_o:\mathcal P\longrightarrow\mathcal O\]

be the forgetting and freeing functors. A PST functorial trajectory is defined by

\[U=\Phi_z(Q_1),\qquad R=\Phi_o(U),\qquad I=\Phi_z(R),\qquad Q_2=\Phi_o(I).\]

Therefore,

\[Q_2=(\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z)(Q_1).\]

The node-type pattern is

\[\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O.\]

If the adjunction \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\) is assumed, then it provides

\[\eta:\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal P}\Longrightarrow\Phi_z\circ\Phi_o,\qquad\varepsilon:\Phi_o\circ\Phi_z\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal O},\]

and the natural bijection

\[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Phi_oP,O)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(P,\Phi_zO).\]

However, the adjunction does not by itself prove that every process has exactly five nodes. That statement requires the PST axioms below.

3. The dynamical arena

Let \(M\) be a smooth state manifold and let \(I=[t_0,t_f]\). Consider the Cauchy problem

\[\dot y(t)=G(y(t),t),\qquad y(t_0)=x\in M.\]

Assume that there exist smooth potentials \(E,S:M\times I\to\mathbb R\) and smooth operators

\[L(y,t):T_y^*M\longrightarrow T_yM,\qquad M(y,t):T_y^*M\longrightarrow T_yM\]

such that

\[G(y,t)=L(y,t)\,dE(y,t)+M(y,t)\,dS(y,t).\]

Then the process is called PST-representable.

Here \(dE_y,dS_y\in T_y^*M\) are covectors, while \(v(y,t)\in T_yM\) is a vector. The operators \(L_y\) and \(M_y\) translate covectors into vectors.

4. PST realization theorem

Theorem 4.1 — PST realization.
Every PST-representable deterministic process admits a canonical PST decomposition

\[x\longrightarrow(dE,dS)\longrightarrow(L,M)\longrightarrow v\longrightarrow y.\]

The decomposition has exactly five nodes and four transitions.

Proof.
By the PST-representability condition,

\[\dot y(t)=L(y(t),t)\,dE(y(t),t)+M(y(t),t)\,dS(y(t),t).\]

Define

\[Q_1=x,\qquad U=(dE,dS),\qquad R=(L,M),\qquad I=v,\qquad Q_2=y.\]

Then the chain

\[x\longrightarrow(dE,dS)\longrightarrow(L,M)\longrightarrow v\longrightarrow y\]

has five nodes and four transitions. The first transition extracts the covector field from the state. The second transition supplies the structure operators. The third transition produces the vector field. The fourth transition integrates the vector field into the trajectory.

If the resulting right-hand side is locally Lipschitz in \(y\), then the Picard–Lindelöf theorem gives a unique local solution satisfying \(y(t_0)=x\). Thus the final node \(y\) is not an additional assumption: it is the integral curve generated by the preceding PST data. \(\square\)

5. Relative uniqueness

Theorem 5.1 — Relative uniqueness.
Fix a PST representation by specifying

\[E,\qquad S,\qquad L,\qquad M.\]

Then the induced PST decomposition

\[x\longrightarrow(dE,dS)\longrightarrow(L,M)\longrightarrow v\longrightarrow y\]

is unique.

Proof.
Once \(E\), \(S\), \(L\), and \(M\) are fixed, the covectors \(dE\) and \(dS\), the structure pair \((L,M)\), and the flow

\[v=L\,dE+M\,dS\]

are uniquely determined. The trajectory \(y\) is then uniquely determined locally by the initial condition \(y(t_0)=x\), provided that the induced vector field is locally Lipschitz in \(y\). \(\square\)

Remark.
Without fixing \(E,S,L,M\), the decomposition is not unique: different potentials and different conductance structures may generate the same trajectory. Therefore, PST uniqueness is always relative to a fixed PST representation.

6. Minimality theorem

Theorem 6.1 — PST grammatical minimality.
Assume that a PST-compatible connected process satisfies the following axioms:

  1. It begins and ends in \(\mathsf O\).
  2. Adjacent nodes have different types.
  3. It contains a structure \(R\) distinct from the initial and terminal states.
  4. It contains a realization \(I\) distinct from \(R\).
  5. Direct transitions \(\mathsf O\to\mathsf O\) and \(\mathsf P\to\mathsf P\) are forbidden.
  6. Removal of any node destroys deterministic reconstructibility of the terminal trajectory.

Then every such process has at least five nodes and four transitions. If it is defined as a PST functorial trajectory, it has exactly five nodes and four transitions.

Proof.
Because the process begins and ends in \(\mathsf O\), and adjacent nodes must alternate types, its type sequence must have the form

\[\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O.\]

A shorter alternating sequence,

\[\mathsf O-\mathsf P-\mathsf O,\]

contains only one intermediate object. It cannot simultaneously contain a structure \(R\) distinct from the endpoints and a realization \(I\) distinct from \(R\). Hence at least four transitions are required. Four transitions generate exactly five nodes. \(\square\)

7. Dissipative specialization

Theorem 7.1 — Onsager specialization.
Assume that the entropy contribution is absent and that the structure operator \(K\) is symmetric and positive definite:

\[K=K^{\mathsf T}>0.\]

If

\[v=-K\,dE,\]

then the process admits the dissipative PST decomposition

\[x\longrightarrow dE\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow v\longrightarrow y.\]

Moreover, along the trajectory,

\[\frac{dE}{dt}=dE(v)=-dE(K\,dE)<0\]

whenever \(dE\ne0\).

Proof.
Because \(K\) is positive definite,

\[dE(K\,dE)>0\]

whenever \(dE\ne0\). Therefore,

\[\frac{dE}{dt}=-dE(K\,dE)<0.\]

Thus the dissipative PST chain has a nontrivial thermodynamic consequence: the potential decreases along the realized motion. \(\square\)

8. Gauge equivalence

Theorem 8.1 — PST gauge preservation.
Let

\[G(y,t)=\frac{\varphi(y,t)}{M(y,t)},\qquad M(y,t)>0.\]

Assume locally that \(\nabla E(y,t)>0\), and define

\[M(y,t)=\frac{1}{\nabla E(y,t)},\qquad\varphi(y,t)=\frac{G(y,t)}{\nabla E(y,t)}.\]

Then

\[\frac{\varphi(y,t)}{M(y,t)}=G(y,t).\]

Moreover, the rescaling

\[(M,\varphi)\mapsto(\lambda M,\lambda\varphi),\qquad\lambda>0,\]

corresponds to the potential rescaling

\[E\mapsto\frac{E}{\lambda},\]

and leaves the trajectory \(y(t)\) invariant.

Proof.
We compute

\[\frac{\varphi}{M}=\frac{G/\nabla E}{1/\nabla E}=G.\]

If \(E\mapsto E/\lambda\), then \(\nabla E\mapsto(1/\lambda)\nabla E\). Hence

\[M\mapsto\lambda M,\qquad\varphi\mapsto\lambda\varphi.\]

Therefore \(\varphi/M=G\) is unchanged, and the solution \(y(t)\) is unchanged. \(\square\)

10. Resource-theoretic interpretation

Resource interpretation.
In a symmetric monoidal resource theory, a catalyst is a resource that participates in a transition and is returned unchanged. The PST minimal structure can therefore be interpreted as

\[Q_1+R_{\min}\longrightarrow Q_2+R_{\min}.\]

This does not mean that the transition is physically free of cost. It means that \(R_{\min}\) is not consumed by the transition, while the transition may be enabled by an external resource gradient.

11. What is not claimed

The theorem does not assert that every conceivable process is PST-representable. It does not assert that every process must have exactly five nodes. It does not derive the five-node structure from the adjunction alone.

The exact result is:

\[\text{PST-representable process}\Longrightarrow\text{canonical five-node PST decomposition}.\]

The converse is false without additional axioms: a five-node diagram alone does not guarantee that an arbitrary empirical process is PST-representable.

12. Meso-palindrome hypothesis

Hypothesis — Meso-transition pattern.
Each PST edge may admit an internal meso-level unfolding of the form

\[\varepsilon\longrightarrow\eta\longrightarrow\eta\longrightarrow\varepsilon.\]

This pattern is not a consequence of the adjunction \(\Phi_o\dashv\Phi_z\). It is a separate PST hypothesis or axiom.

In particular, \(\eta\) and \(\varepsilon\) are natural transformations, not ordinary morphisms of the PST chain. Therefore, the expression

\[\varepsilon\to\eta\to\eta\to\varepsilon\]

must be interpreted at a separate meso-level, with its own objects, morphisms, composition rule, and boundary geometry.

13. Final ALVK statement

Final statement.
For every PST-representable deterministic process, the evolution

\[x\longrightarrow y\]

admits the canonical decomposition

\[x\longrightarrow(dE,dS)\longrightarrow(L,M)\longrightarrow v\longrightarrow y.\]

The decomposition has five nodes and four transitions. Its existence follows from the potential representation of the governing ODE, the GENERIC separation of reversible and irreversible dynamics, and Picard–Lindelöf local existence and uniqueness. Its minimality follows from the PST axioms of type alternation, distinct structure and realization, and deterministic reconstructibility. Its uniqueness is relative to a fixed PST representation.
