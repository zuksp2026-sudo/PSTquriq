Categorical Formalization of the Process–Structure Transition (PST)

Spartac Cebanu · Independent Researcher / QURIQ Framework · January 2026

Abstract. This page combines a categorical proof of the necessity and sufficiency of the Process–Structure Transition (PST) decomposition with its functorial formulation, gauge invariance, and universal property. PST represents a transformation \(\mathbf{x}\to\mathbf{y}\) as a structured chain of five essential components, treated as a composite morphism in a specialized category.

Keywords: category theory, PST decomposition, process morphism, system dynamics, structural adaptation, commutative diagrams, functoriality, gauge equivalence.

1. Introduction

The Process–Structure Transition (PST) formalizes the internal logic of dynamic change. A transformation is not treated as an unstructured “black box,” but as a directed sequence:

$$ \mathbf{x}\xrightarrow{\pi_1}\operatorname{grad}F \xrightarrow{\pi_2}F \xrightarrow{\pi_3}F' \xrightarrow{\pi_4}\mathbf{y}. $$
\(\mathbf{x}\) \(\xrightarrow{\pi_1}\) \(\operatorname{grad}F\) \(\xrightarrow{\pi_2}\) \(F\) \(\xrightarrow{\pi_3}\) \(F'\) \(\xrightarrow{\pi_4}\) \(\mathbf{y}\)

The total transformation is the composite morphism

$$ \mathbf{M}_{xy}=\pi_4\circ\pi_3\circ\pi_2\circ\pi_1. $$

2. The PST category

Definition 2.1 — The PST category \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{PST}}\). \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{PST}}\) is a directed acyclic category (DAC) in which:

  • Objects are state spaces representing the stages of transformation: \(\{\mathbf{x},\operatorname{grad}F,F,F',\mathbf{y}\}\).
  • Morphisms are non-degenerate, causally directed transformations \(\pi_i\), representing physical or logical transition laws.

Definition 2.2 — Process morphism. The total transformation is the composite morphism

$$ \mathbf{M}_{xy}=\pi_4\circ\pi_3\circ\pi_2\circ\pi_1. $$

3. Uniqueness and minimality

Theorem 3.1 — Uniqueness and minimality. The decomposition of \(\mathbf{M}_{xy}\) into the sequence \((\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3,\pi_4)\) is the unique minimal irreducible factorization. Removal of any object results in loss of structural coherence and deterministic reconstructibility.

Proof of sufficiency

By the axioms of category theory, the composition of well-defined morphisms \(\pi_i\) uniquely defines \(\mathbf{M}_{xy}\). Each stage captures a necessary transition—from potential to flux, or from state to gradient—so the complete chain provides the parameters required to map the domain \(\mathbf{x}\) to the codomain \(\mathbf{y}\).

Proof of necessity

Assume that a sub-factorization preserves the identity of \(\mathbf{M}_{xy}\) while omitting at least one intermediate object.

  1. Absence of \(F\) — structure. A shortcut \(\mu:\operatorname{grad}F\to F'\) implies a memoryless system. The object \(F\) internalizes the gradient; without it, the morphism fails to account for the state of accumulation and reduces the process to a static mapping.
  2. Absence of \(\operatorname{grad}F\) — gradient. A morphism \(\mathbf{x}\to F\) lacks a causal trigger. In \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{PST}}\), \(\operatorname{grad}F\) represents differential tension; without it, there is no functorial explanation of why the state \(\mathbf{x}\) should transition into structure \(F\).
  3. Absence of \(F'\) — flow. A morphism \(F\to\mathbf{y}\) ignores temporal realization. Since \(F\) represents potential or structure, it cannot map directly to a terminal state without a rate-of-change component \(F'\).

4. The category of dynamical systems

Definition 4.1 — Category of dynamical systems \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{Dyn}}\). Objects are triples \((M,G,x_0)\), where:

  • \(M\) is a smooth manifold, the state space;
  • \(G:M\times I\to TM\) is a smooth, possibly time-dependent vector field;
  • \(x_0\in M\) is an initial condition.

A morphism

$$ \psi:(M,G,x_0)\longrightarrow(N,H,y_0) $$

is a smooth map \(\psi:M\to N\) such that

$$ \psi(x_0)=y_0, \qquad D\psi\circ G=H\circ\psi. $$

Thus \(\psi\) intertwines the trajectories generated by \(G\) and \(H\).

Remark. Solutions of the ODE

$$ \dot y=G(y,t) $$

are not morphisms in \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{Dyn}}\), but derived objects canonically associated with the triple \((M,G,x_0)\).

5. PST objects and intermediate categories

The PST decomposition associates to each object \((M,G,x_0)\) a chain of structured objects:

$$ x_0 \xrightarrow{\pi_1}\nabla\varphi \xrightarrow{\pi_2}\Phi \xrightarrow{\pi_3}\xi \xrightarrow{\pi_4}y(t). $$

These stages correspond respectively to localization, structural integration, flux extraction, and realization.

  • \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{State}}\): pointed manifolds \((M,x_0)\).
  • \(\mathcal C_{\nabla}\): gradient fields or exact one-forms.
  • \(\mathcal C_{\Phi}\): scalar potentials, modulo additive constants.
  • \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{Flux}}\): time-dependent flux fields governing evolution.
  • \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{Path}}\): realized integral curves with fixed initial data.

6. Functoriality of the PST chain

Theorem 6.1 — Functoriality of PST. There exists a functor

$$ F_{\mathrm{PST}}:\mathcal C_{\mathrm{Dyn}}\longrightarrow\mathcal C_{\mathrm{Path}} $$

defined as the composite

$$ F_{\mathrm{PST}}:=\pi_4\circ\pi_3\circ\pi_2\circ\pi_1, $$

where each transition \(\pi_i\) is functorial, either strictly or up to canonical isomorphism.

Proof

  1. Localization \((\pi_1)\). The map \(x_0\mapsto\nabla\varphi\) lifts a pointed manifold into a canonical differential structure on its cotangent bundle. This defines a functor \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{State}}\to\mathcal C_{\nabla}\), interpretable as a left adjoint to the forgetful functor that discards differential structure.
  2. Structural integration \((\pi_2)\). On simply connected domains, the correspondence between exact gradients and scalar potentials defines an equivalence of categories
$$ \mathcal C_{\nabla}\simeq\mathcal C_{\Phi}, $$

up to additive constants. Hence \(\pi_2\) is functorial up to canonical isomorphism.

  1. Flux extraction \((\pi_3)\). The passage from a potential \(\Phi\) to a flux \(\xi\) is governed by differentiation. Although differentiation is not a strict functor on arbitrary function spaces, in this structured setting it defines a lax functor, preserving composition up to coherence isomorphisms induced by the Leibniz rule.
  2. Realization \((\pi_4)\). Given a flux field \(\xi\) and an initial condition, the map \(\xi\mapsto y(t)\) is uniquely defined by the Picard–Lindelöf theorem. This defines a well-defined functor \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{Flux}}\to\mathcal C_{\mathrm{Path}}\), preserving identities and isomorphisms.
  3. Compositionality. For composable morphisms \(\psi_1,\psi_2\) in \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{Dyn}}\), functoriality of the components implies a canonical isomorphism
$$ F_{\mathrm{PST}}(\psi_2\circ\psi_1) \cong F_{\mathrm{PST}}(\psi_2)\circ F_{\mathrm{PST}}(\psi_1). $$
  1. Identity. The identity object \((M,0,x_0)\) is mapped to the constant trajectory \(y(t)\equiv x_0\). Hence \(F_{\mathrm{PST}}\) preserves identities.

Commutative diagram. Transforming a dynamical system by a morphism \(\psi\) and then realizing its trajectory yields the same result as realizing the trajectory first and then mapping it by the induced path morphism:

$$ \begin{array}{ccc} (M,G,x_0)&\xrightarrow{\psi}&(N,H,y_0)\\ \Big\downarrow{F_{\mathrm{PST}}}&&\Big\downarrow{F_{\mathrm{PST}}}\\ y_1(t)\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{Path}} &\xrightarrow{F_{\mathrm{PST}}(\psi)}& y_2(t)\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{Path}} \end{array} $$

7. Gauge invariance

Corollary 7.1 — Gauge invariance as a natural isomorphism. The PST gauge equivalence class defines a natural isomorphism between PST functors.

Consider the PST gauge transformation

$$ (M,\varphi)\sim(\lambda M,\lambda\varphi), $$

where \(M\) denotes the structural kernel function, not the manifold, and \(\lambda\) is a nowhere-vanishing scalar field. Let \(F_{\mathrm{PST}}^{(1)}\) and \(F_{\mathrm{PST}}^{(\lambda)}\) denote PST functors corresponding to two representatives of the same gauge class. For each object, define a component

$$ \eta_\lambda:F_{\mathrm{PST}}^{(1)}\Rightarrow F_{\mathrm{PST}}^{(\lambda)} $$

by pointwise multiplication by \(\lambda\) on intermediate structures. Since \(\lambda\) cancels in the ratio defining velocity,

$$ \dot y=\frac{\varphi}{M}, $$

all diagrams commute. Therefore, \(\eta_\lambda\) is a natural isomorphism, and the realized trajectory \(y(t)\) is invariant under the choice of gauge representative.

8. Universal property

Theorem 8.1 — Canonical factorization property. The PST decomposition satisfies a universal property: it is the canonical factorization of a dynamical process. Any factorization of the evolution operator compatible with deterministic trajectory reconstruction factors uniquely, up to canonical isomorphism, through the PST chain in the 2-category of structured dynamical systems.

Each PST object

$$ \{x_0,\nabla\varphi,\Phi,\xi,y\} $$

encodes information strictly necessary for deterministic evolution. Removal of any stage destroys either trajectory reconstructibility or functorial consistency. Consequently, PST represents a minimal sufficient structure ensuring both trajectory reconstructibility and categorical coherence of the dynamics.

9. Conclusion

The PST framework provides a categorical description of irreducible dynamic systems. It identifies a minimal sufficient set of objects that ensures deterministic reconstructibility, functorial coherence, gauge invariance, and a canonical factorization property.
