The Canonical Information Chain and Gauge Equivalence

Spartac Cebanu

This page formalizes the operational sequence of a dynamical process. It presents a canonical factorization of scalar first-order ordinary differential equations into a five-element information chain and establishes its relation to a multiplicative gauge class.

Definition: the information chain

Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}\) be an open domain and \(I=[t_0,t_f]\). Consider the scalar Cauchy problem

$$ \dot y(t)=G(y(t),t),\qquad y(t_0)=x\in\Omega, $$

where \(G\in C^1(\Omega\times I)\) is locally Lipschitz in \(y\). The information chain of the process is the ordered five-tuple

$$ \mathcal S_F=\bigl(x,\nabla F,F,F',y\bigr). $$
  • \(x\in\Omega\) is the initial state at \(t_0\).
  • \(F\in C^2(\Omega\times I)\) is a scalar primitive, or potential.
  • \(\nabla F(y,t):=\partial_yF(y,t)\) is the spatial derivative, or gradient.
  • \(F'(t):=\frac{d}{dt}F(y(t),t)\) is the total temporal derivative, or flux, along the trajectory.
  • \(y\in C^1(I,\Omega)\) is the integral curve, or observable trajectory.

The non-degeneracy condition is

$$ \nabla F(y,t)\neq0 \qquad\text{on }\Omega\times I. $$

Theorem: PST factorization and operational irreducibility

The information chain \(\mathcal S_F\) represents a necessary and sufficient data skeleton for the unique deterministic reconstruction of the system’s evolution. Furthermore, the directed sequence

$$ x\longrightarrow\nabla F\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow F'\longrightarrow y $$

is a specific, gauge-fixed representative of the process–structure equivalence class, bridging the geometrical state space and the temporal flux.

Part I: sufficiency

Assume that \(\mathcal S_F\) is given and that \(\nabla F\neq0\). By the chain rule for the total time derivative of the potential \(F\) along the trajectory \(y(t)\),

$$ F'(t)=\nabla F(y(t),t)\cdot\dot y(t)+\partial_tF(y(t),t). $$

Solving algebraically for the local velocity gives

$$ \dot y(t)=\frac{F'(t)-\partial_tF(y(t),t)}{\nabla F(y(t),t)}. $$

Integrating with respect to time and imposing the initial condition \(x\) yields the integral curve

$$ y(t)=x+\int_{t_0}^{t} \frac{F'(s)-\partial_tF(y(s),s)}{\nabla F(y(s),s)}\,ds. $$

Since \(F\in C^2\) and \(\nabla F\neq0\), the integrand is \(C^1\) and locally Lipschitz in \(y\). By the Picard–Lindelöf theorem, the local solution exists, is unique, and extends to \(I\). Thus \(\mathcal S_F\) is sufficient for deterministic path synthesis.

Part II: necessity

The minimality of \(\mathcal S_F\) follows from the fact that removing any single element breaks the injectivity of the mapping from the data set to the trajectory \(y(t)\).

  1. Absence of \(x\): the trajectory is defined only up to an integration constant; distinct initial states can generate different trajectories.
  2. Absence of \(\nabla F\): without the spatial gradient, the local scaling of the potential change relative to spatial displacement is unknown, so \(\dot y\) cannot be calculated.
  3. Absence of \(F\): without the primitive potential, the explicit time derivative \(\partial_tF\) is unavailable, preventing separation of the autonomous structural evolution from movement within the field.
  4. Absence of \(F'\): without the total temporal flux, the actual rate of potential change along the dynamical path is unknown.
  5. Absence of \(y\): without the integral curve, the system lacks a terminal verification state.

Therefore, no proper subset of \(\mathcal S_F\) is informationally complete.

Part III: gauge isomorphism

Let the governing vector field admit a process–structure decomposition of the form

$$ G(y,t)=\frac{\varphi(y,t)}{M(y,t)}, \qquad M(y,t)>0. $$

Assuming locally that \(\nabla F>0\), define

$$ M(y,t):=\frac{1}{\nabla F(y,t)}, \qquad \varphi(y,t):=\frac{G(y,t)}{\nabla F(y,t)}. $$

Then \(M>0\), and the ratio is preserved:

$$ \frac{\varphi(y,t)}{M(y,t)} = \frac{G(y,t)/\nabla F(y,t)}{1/\nabla F(y,t)} =G(y,t). $$

Thus the information chain \(\mathcal S_F\) is a gauge-fixed realization of the dynamics. The structure \(M\) is inversely proportional to the gradient of the potential, while the process \(\varphi\) absorbs temporal flux variations. A scale transformation

$$ (M,\varphi)\mapsto(\lambda M,\lambda\varphi), \qquad\lambda>0, $$ corresponds to choosing a different potential \(F\), while leaving the integral curve \(y(t)\) invariant.

Remark. The theorem mathematically formalizes the Process–Structure Transition (PST). The necessity and sufficiency conditions show that the sequence

$$ x\to\operatorname{grad}F\to F\to F'\to y $$

is not merely a conceptual metaphor, but a minimal, operationally irreducible algebraic skeleton of a deterministic scalar dynamical system.
